1. The Planetary Boundary Layer & Thermal Inversion Model

Winter smog episodes are characterized by calm winds (< 5 km/h) and strong nocturnal radiative cooling of the ground surface. This sets up a positive thermal gradient (dT/dz > 0), suppressing vertical turbulent mixing. Pollutant dispersion is modeled via the Eulerian Box Model:

C_ground(t) = Q_emission / [ u_wind · z_i(t) · W_city ] + C_background

Where z_i(t) is the planetary boundary layer mixing height. During nocturnal inversions, z_i collapses to 80–120m, concentrating exhaust near ground level. Midday solar heating triggers convective thermals, expanding z_i to 800–1200m and diluting ground particulate concentration by up to 8x.

2. Physiological Inhaled Dose & Berkeley Earth Cigarette Equivalence

Human inhaled dose is a function of the minute ventilation rate (V_E):

Dose (µg) = ∫ [ V_E(activity) · C_PM2.5(t) ] dt

At rest or sleeping, V_E ≈ 0.45–0.50 m³/h. During vigorous aerobic exercise, V_E surges to 2.50 m³/h, and oronasal breathing bypasses the upper respiratory tract's natural ciliated filtration. Berkeley Earth equivalence translates microgram-hours to cigarettes:
